    Intent

    This document is the design justification for Rev B of the OSCAR GNSS Receiver PCB. For each block it records the part that was selected, why it was selected, the datasheet numbers that matter, and how much margin the design has against the application. It is written to be read alongside the Flight Controller Design Justification Document, so topics already explained there, such as stackup, JLCPCB capabilities, and TVS fundamentals, are only summarized here.

    The document is paired with the schematic, BOM, and KiCad project files. Where this document and the schematic disagree, the schematic is authoritative for what was actually fabricated; this document captures the reasoning at the time of design.

    Glossary

    Term Definition
    GNSS Global Navigation Satellite System; the umbrella term for GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou.
    L1 The civil GNSS band around 1.575 GHz; GPS L1 is 1575.42 MHz.
    NMEA 0183 Plain-text sentence format, such as $GPGGA, sent by GNSS modules over UART.
    TTFF Time To First Fix: the time from power-up until the receiver reports a position.
    CEP Circular Error Probable: the radius containing 50% of position fixes.
    LNA Low-Noise Amplifier, the first gain stage after the antenna.
    SAW filter Surface Acoustic Wave filter; a sharp band-pass filter.
    Noise Figure (NF) How much a stage degrades signal-to-noise ratio, in dB. Lower is better.
    S-parameters How an RF network reflects (S11, S22) and passes (S21, S12) a signal.
    RHCP Right-Hand Circular Polarization, the polarization transmitted by GNSS satellites.
    Microstrip A trace on an outer layer over a ground plane on the next layer down.
    Via fence Rows of ground vias along an RF trace that keep its fields contained.
    LDO Low-Dropout linear regulator.
    PSRR Power-Supply Rejection Ratio: how well a regulator blocks input ripple.
    TVS Transient Voltage Suppression diode, used for ESD protection.
    ADS Keysight Advanced Design System, the RF simulator used for the front end.
    +A3V3 The flight controller's analog 3.3 V rail, which powers this entire board.

    1. High Level Overview

    1.1 Description of Project

    The OSCAR platform is a senior design project developing a custom flight stack from the board level up. This document covers the GNSS Receiver board, one of three custom PCBs in the stack alongside the Flight Controller (FC) and the Electronic Speed Controller (ESC). It answers two questions for the FC: where are we (X/Y position, velocity, and time from the ST Teseo-LIV3R GNSS module), and which way are we pointing (magnetic heading from the MEMSIC MMC5983MA magnetometer).

    The board is 4-layer, 60 x 60 mm, and mounts directly above the FC on the same 50 x 50 mm M2 hole pattern. It is mounted with its bottom side facing the sky, which puts the ceramic patch antenna on top of the drone while all active circuitry sits underneath, shielded by the board's own ground planes.

    Why does a GNSS board need this much care? By the time a GPS signal reaches the ground it is roughly \(\text{-130 dBm}\), while thermal noise in the 2 MHz GPS bandwidth is about \(\text{-111 dBm}\): \[\Delta P = -130\ \mathrm{dBm} - (-111\ \mathrm{dBm}) \approx -19\ \mathrm{dB}.\] The signal is therefore about 19 dB below the noise floor and is recovered through spread-spectrum correlation gain. Every dB lost in front of the first amplifier is a dB of sensitivity gone for good, driving most of the decisions in Section 3.5.

    1.2 Scope

    This document is a block-by-block design justification for Rev B of the GNSS board.

    • Interconnects to the FC: JST-SH connectors and ESD protection.
    • Magnetometer subsystem: I2C and SPI operation.
    • GNSS receiver system level: UART, supply filtering, LNA regulator, and backup battery.
    • GNSS RF path: antenna, LNA, SAW filter, and Keysight ADS simulation.
    • Layout overview and JLCPCB ordering.

    Out of scope and covered in companion documents are the FC itself, the ESC, and the firmware that parses NMEA and runs compass calibration. Project files and related documentation are available in the OSCAR documentation repository.

    1.3 Block Diagram

    Power comes up from the FC's analog 3.3 V rail through any of the three JST-SH connectors. It feeds the Teseo-LIV3R through a small 27 nH filter and the magnetometer directly. The Teseo passes a filtered copy of its supply out on VCC_RF, and a 2.8 V LDO turns that into a clean rail for the LNA.

    On the RF side the signal path reads left to right: the patch antenna, a DC-blocking capacitor and matching inductor, the BGA725L6 LNA, the B4327 SAW filter, a second DC block, and finally the Teseo RF input. Position comes back to the FC as NMEA over UART (J1). Heading data goes back over I2C (J2) or SPI (J3), depending on which cable is plugged in.

    2. Absolute System Limits

    This section sets the boundaries the board has to live inside. Each limit is the most restrictive rating among the parts it touches.

    2.1 Size and Mounting

    The board is 60 x 60 mm with 5 mm corner radii and uses the same 50 x 50 mm M2 hole pattern as the FC and ESC, so all boards stack directly. Size was the binding constraint on the antenna. ST notes that bigger patch antennas perform better, but a 25 mm patch would not leave room for the connectors, so an 18 mm patch was used. Board size also forced the antenna onto the bottom layer.

    2.3 Power Dissipation

    Everything runs from +A3V3 at 3.3 V. Typical numbers use datasheet typicals. Maximum numbers use datasheet maximums where they exist. The Teseo has no published maximum, so it gets a 20% allowance.

    IC Typical current Typical power Maximum current Maximum power
    Teseo-LIV3R (GPS + GLONASS tracking) 44.5 mA 147 mW 53.4 mA 176 mW
    BGA725L6 LNA (at 2.8 V) 3.6 mA 10.1 mW 4.5 mA 12.6 mW
    TLV75528 LDO (headroom loss) — 1.9 mW — 2.4 mW
    MMC5983MA magnetometer 0.45 mA 1.5 mW 4.6 mA 15.1 mW

    • The Teseo is rated at 93.4 mW with VCC = 2.1 V, so its current is \[I = \frac{93.4\ \mathrm{mW}}{2.1\ \mathrm{V}} = 44.5\ \mathrm{mA},\qquad P = 3.3\ \mathrm{V}\cdot 44.5\ \mathrm{mA} \approx 147\ \mathrm{mW}.\]
    • The magnetometer maximum is continuous 1 kHz sampling, scaled from the datasheet because its current is proportional to measurement rate.
    • Pull-ups draw current only while a line is held low: 0.7 mA each through 4.7 kΩ. At about 0.2 W worst case across the board, no heatsinking is needed. The LDO rises only about \[ \Delta T = 2.4\ \mathrm{mW}\cdot 231\ ^\circ\mathrm{C/W} \approx 0.5\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}. \]

    3. Block by Block Verification

    3.1 JST Connectors

    The board uses JST SH-series (1.0 mm pitch) right-angle headers, the same family the FC uses. They are standard on drone peripherals, small enough to fit, and have a friction lock so they stay seated under vibration. Every connector carries its own 3.3 V and ground, so the board powers up from whichever cable is plugged in.

    Signal names are written from the FC's point of view, so GNSS_UART_RX is the line the FC receives on (the Teseo TX pin). Because both boards use the same convention, a straight 1:1 JST cable works on all three connectors.

    J1: GNSS UART (mates with FC J6)

    Pin Signal Direction Description
    1 GND — Return / reference
    2 GNSS_UART_RX GNSS → FC NMEA out of the Teseo (TX, pin 2)
    3 GNSS_UART_TX FC → GNSS Configuration commands into the Teseo (RX, pin 3)
    4 +A3V3 FC → GNSS FC-to-GNSS board supply

    J2: Magnetometer I2C (mates with FC J9)

    Pin Signal Direction Description
    1 GND — Return / reference
    2 MAG_SCL FC → MAG FC I2C clock
    3 MAG_SDA Bidirectional I2C data
    4 +A3V3 FC → GNSS GNSS board supply

    J3: Magnetometer SPI (mates with FC J8)

    Pin Signal Direction Description
    1 GND — Return / reference
    2 MAG_CS FC → MAG Chip select (active low)
    3 MAG_SCLK FC → MAG SPI clock
    4 MAG_MISO MAG → FC Sensor data out (MMC5983MA SDO, pin 5)
    5 MAG_MOSI FC → MAG Sensor data in (MMC5983MA SDI, pin 16)

    3.2 TVS Diodes

    The key TVS parameters (stand-off voltage, clamp voltage, dynamic resistance, and line capacitance) are explained in Section 4.1.1 of the FC Design Justification Document. Every pin that leaves this board, except ground, gets one TI ESD351, for 11 total (D1–D11).

    The ESD351 is used instead of the ESD601 used on the FC because every line on the GNSS board is 3.3 V, and the protected parts have absolute maximums of only 3.6–4.3 V. Its 3.6 V stand-off sits above the rail without leaking and it clamps at only 6.5 V.

    Parameter Value Requirement
    Reverse stand-off voltage (VRWM) 3.6 V Above the 3.3 V rail (9% margin)
    Breakdown voltage (VBR) 4.5 V min Will not conduct during normal operation
    Clamping voltage 6.5 V at 16 A As low as practical for 3.3 V parts
    Line capacitance 1.8 pF typ Negligible on 400 kHz I2C, 10 MHz SPI, or UART
    IEC 61000-4-2 contact / air ±30 kV / ±30 kV ±8 kV / ±15 kV required (Level 4)

    3.3 Magnetometer (I2C and SPI)

    The MMC5983MA was selected to answer which way the vehicle is pointing. The magnetometer was moved off the FC because motor and ESC currents directly underneath the FC could interfere with Earth's field. Mounted on the GNSS board, it sits as far from those currents as the stack allows.

    Parameter Value Why it matters
    Resolution / range 18-bit, ±8 G Plenty of range around Earth's ~0.5 G field
    Noise 0.4 mG RMS Supports 0.5–1° heading accuracy
    Output data rate Up to 1 kHz Faster than the flight loop needs
    SET/RESET Built-in degaussing coil Removes offset drift and magnetization from strong motor fields
    Interfaces I2C 400 kHz or SPI 10 MHz Either FC port can be used
    Supply 2.8–3.6 V, 0.45 mA typ Runs directly from +A3V3

    The interface is set by the chip-select pin. R1 (4.7 kΩ) pulls CS high, putting the sensor in I2C mode. When the FC is plugged into J3 and drives CS low, the sensor switches to SPI. The mode follows whichever cable is plugged in; no mode switch is needed.

    The FC already has 4.7 kΩ pull-ups on its magnetometer I2C bus, so local pull-ups R2 and R3 are open by default. JP2 and JP3 connect them if the board is used with a host that has no pull-ups. With approximately 35 pF of cable and pin capacitance:

    \[ t_r \approx 0.8473 \cdot R \cdot C = 0.8473 \cdot 4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\cdot 35\ \mathrm{pF} \approx 140\ \mathrm{ns} < 300\ \mathrm{ns} \]

    This is below the I2C Fast-mode limit. If JP2 and JP3 are bridged while connected to the FC, R2 and R3 are parallel with the FC pull-ups, producing an effective 2.35 kΩ and a rise time of about 70 ns, so accidentally closing the jumpers does not break anything.

    Supporting parts are C8 (4.7 µF), which decouples VDD, and C7 (10 µF) on the CAP pin, which is the energy reservoir for the SET/RESET pulse and is placed close to the pin as required by MEMSIC.

    3.4 GNSS Receiver System Level

    The TESEO-LIV3R was selected to provide X/Y position. It is a complete GNSS receiver in a 9.7 x 10.1 mm module with its own TCXO, real-time clock, and ROM firmware. It powers up and starts outputting NMEA without needing firmware to be flashed.

    Parameter Value
    Constellations GPS + GLONASS by default, or GPS + BeiDou, plus QZSS and SBAS
    Tracking sensitivity −163 dBm with the external LNA used here
    Position accuracy < 1.5 m CEP
    Fix rate Up to 10 Hz
    TTFF < 32 s cold start, < 1.5 s hot start
    Power (tracking) ≈ 147 mW at 3.3 V

    Unused pins (1PPS, Wake-Up, SYS_RSTn, Ant_OFF, I2C, and reserved pins) are left floating as the datasheet instructs.

    3.4.1 UART Protocol

    The Teseo communicates with the FC over a 3.3 V UART carrying NMEA 0183 text sentences. It defaults to 9600 baud, which is sufficient at a 1 Hz fix rate. At 10 Hz, the estimated data rate is:

    \[ 5\ \mathrm{sentences}\cdot 80\ \mathrm{characters}\cdot 10\ \mathrm{Hz} \cdot 10\ \frac{\mathrm{bits}}{\mathrm{character}} \approx 40\ \mathrm{kbit/s}. \]

    This is about four times what 9600 baud can carry, so FC firmware should switch the Teseo to 115200 baud (approximately 11.5 kB/s) before enabling 10 Hz updates. Both ends use the same 3.3 V rail, so no level shifting is needed.

    3.4.2 General Filtering on VDD

    ST's hardware manual (UM2231) specifies a 27 nH series inductor in the Teseo supply because noise on VCC directly degrades sensitivity. The design uses L1 (Murata LQG15HN27N) with a 10 nF capacitor on each side (C4 and C5), forming a small pi filter.

    BT1 is a CR2032 coin cell on the Teseo VBAT pin. While VBAT is powered, the module keeps its real-time clock, satellite data, and configuration. This preserves the difference between a < 1.5 s hot start and a < 32 s cold start. In standby the Teseo draws only about 6 µA from the cell, so a 225 mAh CR2032 lasts years. There is no charging path from the 3.3 V rail into the battery, which matters because a CR2032 cannot sink current.

    3.4.3 2V8 LDO Voltage Regulator for LNA

    The LNA is powered from the Teseo VCC_RF pin, which ST describes as a filtered copy of VCC intended for an external LNA or active antenna. A TI TLV75528 LDO drops this to 2.8 V. The regulator provides absolute-maximum margin, adds approximately 52 dB of PSRR to the Teseo filtering, and avoids the switching noise a buck would introduce near the RF signal. With a load under 5 mA and a 0.5 V drop, the LDO wastes only about 2 mW.

    Parameter Rating Application
    Input range 1.45–5.5 V VCC_RF ≈ 3.27 V
    Dropout 300 mV at 500 mA ≈ 3 mV at 4.5 mA; 470 mV headroom
    Output current 500 mA LNA draws 3.6 mA typ (< 1%)
    Accuracy ±1% 2.77–2.83 V, inside the LNA's 1.5–3.3 V range
    Capacitors ≥ 1 µF in / out C9 and C10 = 4.7 µF

    VCC_RF turns off when the Teseo enters hardware standby, so the LNA powers down automatically.

    3.5 GNSS RF Path

    The RF chain is the antenna, a DC block and matching inductor, the BGA725L6 LNA, the B4327 SAW filter, a second DC block, and the Teseo RF input. This is the same front end ST uses in its own Teseo-LIV3 reference design (UM2231).

    3.5.1 Antenna Choice

    The Linx ANT-GNSSCP-TH18L1 is an 18 x 18 x 4 mm ceramic patch antenna. It is soldered by a single feed pin and held with an adhesive pad.

    Parameter GPS L1 GLONASS
    Peak gain 5.1 dBi 4.0 dBi
    Efficiency 66% 65%
    VSWR (max) 2.8 2.9
    Polarization RHCP RHCP

    GNSS satellites transmit right-hand circularly polarized signals. Reflections from the ground or drone frame flip to left-hand polarization, so an RHCP patch naturally rejects much of this multipath. A chip antenna is smaller but linearly polarized and omnidirectional, so it also picks up reflections.

    The passive antenna was chosen primarily for the design challenge and the opportunity to simulate, lay out, and assemble part of the RF path. The antenna is on the bottom layer because of board size; this also puts the board's ground planes between the antenna and noisy digital parts. The board is mounted bottom-up so the antenna faces the sky.

    Linx measured this antenna on a 70 x 70 mm ground plane while this board is 60 x 60 mm. The smaller ground plane can shift tuning slightly and cost some gain.

    3.5.2 LNA Choice

    The Infineon BGA725L6 is a silicon-germanium LNA built specifically for GNSS (1550–1615 MHz) in a 0.7 x 1.1 mm package.

    Parameter Rating Application
    Gain 20 dB Makes losses after it nearly irrelevant
    Noise figure 0.65 dB Sets the noise figure of the whole receiver
    Supply current 3.6 mA Small power cost
    Output match Internally matched to 50 Ω Connects directly to the SAW filter
    Stability μ > 1, 20 MHz–10 GHz Will not oscillate

    The Friis equation shows why the LNA comes first:

    \[ F_{\mathrm{total}} = F_1 + \frac{F_2-1}{G_1} + \frac{F_3-1}{G_1G_2}+\cdots \]

    With the LNA first, its 0.65 dB noise figure sets the system. Putting the SAW filter first would add approximately 1 dB loss directly, giving roughly 1.9 dB total instead of 1.0 dB. The tradeoff is that the LNA sees strong out-of-band signals before filtering, which is why it runs at 2.8 V for better linearity and the ELRS antenna should be kept at least 20–30 cm away.

    Infineon's application circuit uses a series inductor to match the LNA input. Here L2 = 8.2 nH was re-tuned in ADS to absorb antenna pad parasitics. C3 (27 pF) is a DC block: at 1.575 GHz its reactance is approximately:

    \[ |X_C|=\frac{1}{2\pi f C} =\frac{1}{2\pi(1.575\ \mathrm{GHz})(27\ \mathrm{pF})} \approx 3.7\ \Omega. \]

    3.5.3 SAW Filter Choice

    The RF360 (Qualcomm) B39162B4327P810 is a 1.4 x 1.1 mm SAW filter that needs no matching components. It passes the three bands the Teseo can track with about 1 dB loss and rejects everything else.

    Band Rejection / loss What lives there
    824–925 MHz 43 dB Cellular, ELRS 868/915 MHz
    1710–1910 MHz 46–48 dB Cellular uplink (DCS/PCS)
    2400–2500 MHz 46 dB ELRS 2.4 GHz, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth
    GPS L1 passband 1.0 dB loss The desired signal

    The SAW cannot tolerate DC voltage, so C2 (27 pF) sits between it and the Teseo RF input.

    3.5.4 Simulated Results

    The complete RF path, from antenna pad to Teseo RF input pad, was simulated in Keysight ADS using real copper geometry, Murata S-parameter models for the 0402 capacitors and inductor, Infineon's 2.8 V LNA model, and the JLC04161H-7628 substrate (\(H=0.2104\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(\epsilon_r=4.4\)).

    Metric at 1.575 GHz Simulated Target
    Gain (S21) 17.75 dB LNA 20 dB minus SAW and matching losses
    Input match (S11) −17.4 dB Better than −10 dB
    Output match (S22) −20.4 dB Better than −10 dB
    Reverse isolation (S12) −38.2 dB Better than −30 dB
    Noise figure 1.01 dB As low as possible
    Stability (Mu1) > 1 from 10 MHz–4 GHz > 1 everywhere

    A hand sanity check uses 0.2 dB matching loss, the LNA (0.65 dB, 20 dB gain), the SAW (1 dB), and an assumed 6 dB noise figure for the Teseo internal front end:

    \[ F=1.047+0.169+0.003+0.039=1.258 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad NF\approx 1.0\ \mathrm{dB}. \]

    This agrees with ADS. Without the external LNA, the same chain would be around 7 dB, so the front end buys about 6 dB of sensitivity, which is the margin assumed by ST's −163 dBm tracking figure.

    4. Layout Overview

    4.1 Board Stackup (4-layer)

    The board uses the JLC04161H-7628 stackup, with dielectric thicknesses locked in KiCad so the ADS substrate matches what JLCPCB builds.

    Layer Purpose
    F.Cu (L1) All components, RF path, signal, and GND pour
    In1.Cu (L2) Solid GND, reference plane under RF path
    In2.Cu (L3) Power and magnetometer routing plus GND pour
    B.Cu (L4) Solid GND, ground plane for patch antenna

    4.2 RF Layout

    The RF parts are placed in a straight line in signal order, pad-to-pad, with as little copper between them as possible. The top-layer ground pour is kept away from the RF strip, so every piece is a true microstrip over solid ground on L2.

    A 50 Ω microstrip on this stackup would be about 0.38 mm wide, while the pad-width copper used is about 0.62 mm, or roughly 37 Ω.

    Trace width Impedance
    0.20 mm ≈ 68 Ω
    0.38 mm ≈ 50 Ω
    0.62 mm (as built) ≈ 37 Ω

    At 1.575 GHz on this board, the wavelength is about 102 mm and every RF segment is 1.2 mm or shorter, less than 1/80 of a wavelength. A line this short does not behave like a transmission line, and ADS already accounts for the copper parasitics:

    \[ \lambda=\frac{c}{f\sqrt{\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}}} =\frac{300\ \mathrm{mm}}{1.575\sqrt{3.47}} \approx 102\ \mathrm{mm}. \]

    Two staggered rows of ground vias run along each side of the RF path at 1.0 mm pitch, following the 5 × dielectric-height rule: \[5\cdot 0.21\ \mathrm{mm}\approx 1\ \mathrm{mm}.\] This is tighter than the approximately 5 mm (\(\lambda/20\)) needed at L1, keeping RF fields contained and tying all four ground layers together.

    5. Ordering for JLCPCB

    The board is ordered bare from JLCPCB and assembled by hand. Parts come from DigiKey, except the Teseo-LIV3R, which comes from LCSC.

    Option Setting
    Layers 4
    Dimensions 60 x 60 mm
    Thickness 1.6 mm
    Impedance control Yes, JLC04161H-7628
    Outer / inner copper 1 oz / 0.5 oz
    Surface finish ENIG (optional)
    Via covering Tented
    Minimum via hole / diameter 0.25 mm / 0.55 mm
    Solder mask / silkscreen Black / White
    Stencil Yes, top side

    ENIG is preferred over HASL because the SAW filter, LNA, and magnetometer have pads underneath packages, with the smallest only 0.7 x 1.1 mm. HASL leaves slightly domed pads that can make these parts tilt or bridge; ENIG is flat. A stencil is needed because these parts cannot be soldered with an iron.

    • Reflow all top-side parts first. Fit the antenna last by hand because its adhesive pad is not rated for reflow.
    • The SAW filter (MSL 2a), Teseo, and magnetometer (MSL 3) are moisture sensitive; keep them sealed until use.
    • At first power-up, expect about 45–50 mA from 3.3 V and exactly 2.8 V on the LNA rail.

    Important: Before submitting a board to JLCPCB, use its Design for Manufacturing (DFM) tool to validate that the board meets specifications before purchasing.

    6. Testing Results

    After assembling the PCB, DC continuity tests found no shorts after the reflow oven and small rework. The next step was validating that the 2.8 V regulator could supply the LNA.

    RF validation is difficult at microwave frequencies. The design operates at 1.575 GHz, so a standard oscilloscope measurement is not valid; a vector network analyzer (VNA) is required. The carrier wave is also far below the noise floor, while the LibreVNA has a minimum noise measurement of −100 dB.

    These measurements do not provide a clear quantitative answer on LNA performance; that would require a full two-port measurement with a proper UFL connection at the antenna feed point into the LNA. They do show that the RF chain is connected and amplifying the band of interest, which is sufficient before placing the ST receiver and moving on.
